# The step grid's period cap, swept on both populations it governs

Generated and asserted by `grid-cap-sweep.test.ts`, driven by
`scripts/roll-cap-sweep.mjs grid` (#1041, epic #1007). Raw per-ask rows:
`.grid-cap-runs/cap-<N>.json`.

This is the second half of `LEAF_PROJECT_BARS`. The first is `ROLL-CAP-SWEEP.md`, and the
two documents are deliberately separate because **the two halves of that constant do not
behave alike** — see *Why the roll's reading does not transfer* below before quoting
anything from the other one here.

## Why this was built

`LEAF_PROJECT_BARS.grid` has been **12** since the constant was born at #991. Unlike the
roll's 4, that value was never gated. Its justification lived in a doc comment on the
constant, as a single figure:

> GRID 12. Worth **+17 writer-reach over the 1500-unit corpus (109 → 126)**.

That figure was taken twice and was wrong both times it was checked.

| taken | recorded | on |
|---|---|---|
| #991 | +9 (95 → 104) | an oracle that probed cycle 0 and compared onsets only |
| #1038 | +17 (109 → 126) | after the probe widening (#1022) and the duration axis (#1026) |
| #1041 | *see the generated table below* | this tree, after the corpus widening at #1242 |

Those two rows are DEAD figures, kept because the trap is worth leaving visible. **Today's
row is deliberately not written out**: it is the one this section would otherwise be
transcribing, and transcribing it here — in the document whose whole argument is that the
figures belong in a generated block — is the defect rather than a smaller version of it.

⚠⚠ **The headline stayed exact while both figures under it drifted, and that is the whole
reason this document exists.** The gain is `+17` in both the #1038 row and the current
reading, and the endpoints agree in neither. A delta is precisely the shape that survives a
population change — #1242 moved both terms by nearly the same amount — so the number most
likely to be quoted is the number least likely to look stale. A reader who confirmed "+17"
against this tree would have confirmed the one part of the line that drift preserves, and
walked away having certified two wrong endpoints.

⚠ There is a second trap on the same comment: its dead cap-4 projection collides with a
reading that is still live in the sweep. Searching for stale copies of a figure by its
digits pairs the wrong two things. Enumerate by meaning.

The comment no longer carries the number. It points here, exactly as its roll half already
pointed at `ROLL-CAP-SWEEP.md`.

## The two populations

The same carve the roll sweep uses, and for the same reason.

| | population | who reaches the leaf writer |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | core-**REFUSED** (`parseStepGridCore` says no) | production, today, as the fallback |
| **B** | core-**SERVED** (`parseStepGridCore` says yes) | nobody today — only after the core is deleted (#1012) |

`writer-reach.test.ts`'s population is one line — `if (core.ok) continue` — and the writer
census's is its complement. **A gate defined as another gate's complement carries a
population restriction that none of its numbers mention.** Reading one population's answer
as general is the error that set the *roll's* cap to 4, and it is cheap enough to repeat
that this sweep reports the two separately and never nets them.

Their sizes are generated below rather than written here.

## Why the roll's reading does not transfer

The roll sweep records, in its own floor comment, that **its** population-B floor "is not a
guard on the constant this file sweeps". That sentence is true there and **false here**,
and copying it across would understate a guard that works.

The mechanism is legible rather than coincidental. This cap governs only the **leaf**
writer; the element writer's own bound never moves in either sweep. What decides whether a
population feels the cap is therefore what share of its opened views the leaf writer
serves:

| | opened views | leaf-served | does the cap reach this population? |
|---|---|---|---|
| roll, population B | 415 | **16** | barely — B reads the same transfer count at caps 4, 3, 2 **and** 1 |
| grid, population B | 820 | **74** | yes — B moves across the swept range |

⚠ The grid row's two figures are also produced by the generated block above, which is the
copy to trust; they are repeated here only because the comparison is the point of the
section. The roll row comes from `ROLL-CAP-SWEEP.md`'s own run and is pinned in that
gate's population-B floor comment — it is a cross-reference, not a reading this file takes.

So both of this surface's floors carry a real paired differential, where the roll could
only build one. **Sibling surfaces do not share a constant's sensitivity even where they
share the constant.** Every claim in this document was measured on the grid; none of it was
reasoned across from the neighbour.

⚠ One asymmetry inside this surface, stated rather than buried: population A's floor is
caught by the very next value the constant can take, and population B's is not — B is
unmoved one step down and only moves two steps down. Both floors are pinned to their
observations; B's simply cannot detect the smallest possible regression. That is a
property of the population, not slack in the floor.

## What the floors are, and what they are not

Both floors live in `grid-cap-sweep.test.ts` and both are pinned to the observation, with
the regression that reddens each one named in the comment beside it. A floor sitting above
a comfortable margin is not a weaker floor by a proportion — it is a floor that has stopped
catching one specific regression, and which one is not recoverable from the margin.

Population A's floor is also `writer-reach.test.ts`'s `FLOOR_STEP`, and the two must
agree: two gates disagreeing about the same number over the same population would mean one
of them is measuring something else. Population B's is cross-checked against the writer
census's grid control arm in `P6-CAP12.json`, which reaches the same asks-and-transfers
pair by an entirely different route.

---

## The decision

**The shipped 12 stands, and it is now gated rather than asserted.** What changed at #1041
is not the value — it has been 12 since #991 — but that the reasoning for it is
re-derivable. The gain the comment claimed is real and is reproduced here on the current
corpus; what was never true is that anyone would have noticed if it had stopped being.

The residual question is unchanged and belongs to #1012: population B is a counterfactual
until the syntactic core is deleted, and if that happens the two populations merge and this
cap's justification has to be re-derived on the merged one. The sweep is built so that
re-derivation is a command rather than a project.
